The problem

Nobody knows what they already own.

The average person is in seven or eight loyalty programs and can't name the balance of one. Points expire. Offers lapse. Free food goes uneaten. Not because people don't care — because nothing tells them.

Loss, not balance

Other apps show you a number. Purq shows you a deadline. The home screen leads with what disappears first, because that's the only thing worth interrupting you for.

One honest total

Points, stars, punches and offers, all converted to what they're actually worth in dollars. Estimates are labelled as estimates. We never inflate the number to look impressive.

It finds you

A Wallet pass on your Lock Screen when you're near the store. You don't have to remember to open Purq — that's rather the point of it.

How it works

Three steps, then it runs itself.

Forward your reward emails

You get a private Purq address. Set one filter and the promos, receipts and expiry warnings merchants already send you get read for value, codes and dates. We never touch the rest of your inbox — we only see what you forward.

Set your balances once

Type in what you've already got, or scan the points screen in a merchant's app to save the typing. After that your forwarded receipts keep it current on their own.

Get told before it's gone

One notification when something's about to expire. A Wallet pass that appears when you're near the store. Then you go use it — in the merchant's own app, the way they intended.

Being straight with you

What Purq deliberately doesn't do.

Most apps in this category ask for something they shouldn't. Here's where we drew the lines, and why.

We never ask for merchant passwords

No Chipotle login, no Starbucks login, nothing to leak. Other trackers store those on their servers. There is no merchant credential in Purq to steal, because we never designed a place to put one.

We never read your inbox

No Gmail sign-in, no mailbox access. You forward what you want us to see, to an address that's yours alone. Works with Outlook and iCloud too.

We don't fake a barcode

Where a merchant's code is genuinely yours, we show it. Where the reward lives inside their app, we hand you off and say so. One failure at a counter is worse than an extra tap.

We tell you when we're guessing

Some values are estimates and some dates are read by a machine. Those are marked, and they're kept out of your headline total until you confirm them.

Questions

The ones worth asking.

Is it free?

Yes. The tracker costs us nothing to run, so it costs you nothing to use. If we ever charge, it'll be for something extra, and the thing you signed up for will stay free.

Which programs will work?

Anything that emails you, which is most of them. Each one needs a parser written for its particular email format, so we're starting with the ones people ask for most — Chipotle, Starbucks, Chick-fil-A, Dunkin', Panera, Sephora — and adding from the waitlist requests. Coverage grows as we write parsers, not as you add accounts.

Do you sell my data?

No, and the business model is the reason we can say that plainly: we plan to earn from merchants paying for offers you choose to use. Nothing about you gets sold, now or then. The privacy page says the same thing in more words, including exactly what this website holds today.

Android?

Not yet. iPhone first, properly, because the Wallet and Lock Screen features are most of what makes Purq useful and they don't port cleanly.

When does it launch?

We're in build — nothing has shipped yet. Join the list and you'll get a TestFlight invite before the App Store release. Early testers genuinely shape what ships.
